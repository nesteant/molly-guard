# What this is responsible for

How intent becomes accumulated truth. One unit of intent is drafted as four documents, moved
through a sequence of states, and — eventually — folded into the knowledge base.

The inversion everything here rests on: **the change has the lifecycle, not the
specification.** A document that is simultaneously "what we believe" and "a draft somebody is
editing" cannot be read by anybody, so the two are separated and only one of them moves.

What that costs, and what it buys, is the record. A state is folded from an append-only ledger
rather than stored, so advancing a change does not touch its text; the `state:` in the document
is a projection, and it is checked against the ledger rather than trusted.

# Where the edge is

**Deciding whether a move is allowed.** The sequence here is an order, not a set of permitted
edges: what a move requires — a design present, work claimed, evidence that held — is policy,
and policy belongs to a slice or to whatever orchestrates the work. This capability owns the
vocabulary and the record, and refuses only on the argument: a state that does not exist, a
change that does not exist.

**Doing the work.** Claiming a task, implementing it and producing evidence belong to whoever
or whatever runs the work. What lives here is the bundle such a runner writes into.

**Reading the prose.** Nothing here parses a body. What the tool reads is the frontmatter and
the ledger, which is what leaves the text in whatever language and whatever form its author
chose.

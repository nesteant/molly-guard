# What this is responsible for

The shape of a corpus: the areas it divides into, how a document is named, how it is filed, and
what a reference between two documents is allowed to be.

**The path is the id.** `capabilities/billing` is the capability named `billing`, and no index
has to be consulted to know that — the containing directory already says what kind of thing a
document is, so the id does not repeat it. A name is ASCII, minted once from whatever language
its author was writing in, and never touched by a translator, which is what lets one document
be cited by the same name in every language it exists in.

**A grouping is how a corpus stays readable.** A corpus stops fitting in one sitting long
before it stops being worked on, and what makes it readable again is reading a slice. The slice
is a capability, and it works because a document declares which one it belongs to rather than
because of where somebody happened to put a folder.

Also here: what a scan of an area reports, and the rule that what cannot be read is said out
loud rather than skipped.

# Where the edge is

**What a document says.** This capability owns where a document lives, what it is called and
what may point at it. It has no opinion about the text, and reading the prose belongs to
nothing at all.

**A lifecycle.** What is filed here is current or it is not; anything that moves through states
is a change, and belongs to the change flow. That edge is why creating a capability records
nothing: the ledger is a record of what happened to things in flight.

**Repair.** Nothing here rewrites a reference to keep it valid, and nothing renames on somebody
else's behalf. A reference that has stopped resolving is reported, because the alternative —
editing documents nobody asked to change — is how a tidying operation silently revokes a
review.
